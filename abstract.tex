\chapter*{Abstract}

Automatic modulation classification (AMC) is an important receiver function in
cognitive radio, spectrum monitoring, adaptive wireless communication, and
interference analysis. A Phase 1 implementation of this project developed a
compact convolutional neural network (CNN) that classified raw in-phase and
quadrature (I/Q) samples from the RadioML 2016.10a dataset. The Phase 1
classifier achieved 53.94\% accuracy on a held-out test set, but its output
was not connected to a complete communication receiver.

This Phase 2 work extends the classifier into an intelligent adaptive digital
communication system. The proposed system generates random information bits,
maps them to BPSK, QPSK, 8-PSK, or 16-QAM symbols, transmits them through an
AWGN and block-Rayleigh channel with carrier phase and frequency offsets, and
performs synchronization and channel equalization at the receiver. The
modulation decision is then used to select the demodulator automatically.
Unlike the Phase 1 study, the evaluation measures communication-level
quantities: bit error rate (BER), symbol error rate (SER), packet success
rate, effective throughput, and modulation-decision accuracy.

In the final experiment, 200 frames were evaluated at each SNR from -4 dB to
16 dB. At 16 dB, the adaptive receiver achieved 0.095\% BER, 0.172\% SER,
100\% modulation-decision accuracy, 75.5\% packet success, and 1.888
bits/symbol effective throughput. At 10 dB, it achieved 4.69\% BER and 99.5\%
decision accuracy. These results demonstrate the new communication-system
contribution of Phase 2, while also showing the low-SNR region in which
modulation ambiguity causes packet failures. The report presents the
communication theory, system architecture, software implementation, training
parameters, measured results, limitations, and future SDR-based extensions.
